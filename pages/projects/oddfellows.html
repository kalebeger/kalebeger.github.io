Education × Entrepreneurship

Meet Oddfellows.

Making business education more approachable, engaging, and accessible.

01

The Idea Behind Oddfellows

Oddfellows is an interactive business education platform that I began developing during my fall break in October 2026. The idea had been on my mind for a while, but I wanted to challenge myself to take it beyond just an idea and turn it into something people could eventually use.

Through my own experiences studying business, I've developed an appreciation for how useful business concepts can be, regardless of someone's academic background or career interests. I wanted to explore the possibility of creating a platform that could introduce these concepts in a way that felt less overwhelming and more engaging.

My goal with Oddfellows is to eventually create something that benefits not only business students, but also individuals who haven't pursued a business degree or attended college.

02

How Oddfellows Works

Inspired by the five majors within Bucknell University's Freeman College of Management, Oddfellows introduces users to Accounting, Business Analytics, Finance, Management, and Marketing.

Rather than presenting information through traditional lectures or lengthy readings, the platform uses short, interactive lessons to help users build their understanding of different business concepts.

Each subject has its own original character: Ledger the tortoise represents Accounting, Byte the Boston Terrier represents Business Analytics, Buck the deer represents Finance, Marshal the lion represents Management, and Marky the chameleon represents Marketing.

Users can select a primary learning pathway while exploring other subjects based on their interests. The prototype also incorporates achievements and character-based progression to make the experience more engaging and encourage users to continue learning.

The working prototype currently features 50 interactive lessons across the five business subjects.

Interactive Lessons

I wanted Oddfellows to give people opportunities to work with business concepts rather than only read about them. In this Business Analytics example, users interpret a chart showing notebook sales and choose an answer based on the information presented. A supporting data table offers another way to examine the same figures. The lesson interface organizes the experience into Learn, Practice, Apply, Review, and Complete steps. This illustrates the approach I'm exploring: breaking business topics into smaller activities that ask users to engage with the material and practice a specific skill.

Learning Progression

Oddfellows organizes its lessons into five subject pathways, giving users a structure to follow while they explore their interests. The Marketing pathway shown here moves through Foundations, Building Skills, and Applying Knowledge, with individual lessons arranged along a visual map. Lesson states distinguish what is available from what remains locked, while prerequisites help communicate the order of progression. I see this structure as a way to make a broad subject feel more manageable. Users can see where they're starting, what comes next, and how individual topics fit into a larger learning journey.

03

Building Oddfellows

One of the most interesting parts of developing Oddfellows has been learning how to turn an idea into a working product.

With the help of AI-assisted development tools, I've been able to build and refine the platform while making decisions about its design, learning experience, characters, and overall direction.

This project has also introduced me to areas of product development that I hadn't previously explored in depth. I've spent time thinking about how users would navigate the platform, what would make lessons engaging, and how to balance the educational purpose of Oddfellows with a more creative and interactive experience.

Although there's still plenty of work to be done, developing the prototype has been a valuable opportunity to explore entrepreneurship and apply my interests in business, education, and technology.

Achievements

The achievement system recognizes milestones connected to learning and exploration. Examples shown here include completing a first unique lesson, meeting an Oddfellow, and discovering all five founding characters. Other milestones remain visibly locked until their requirements are met, such as completing lessons across multiple disciplines or earning learning XP. My intention is to give users a sense of progress beyond finishing an individual activity, while keeping that recognition connected to their participation in the learning experience rather than making rewards the main focus.

Character Progression

The five original Oddfellows characters give each business subject a recognizable guide: Ledger for Accounting, Byte for Business Analytics, Buck for Finance, Marshal for Management, and Marky for Marketing. Their collection cards connect those identities to learning progress by displaying individual levels and subject XP. In the example shown, Buck has earned Finance XP while the other characters remain at their starting levels. Character progression uses the same learning points earned through lessons, rather than a separate currency. I wanted the characters to be connected to the educational experience, not simply appear as illustrations beside it.

04

Looking Ahead

Oddfellows is still an ongoing project, and I'm continuing to explore ways to improve the experience.

Some of my next priorities include refining the lessons, improving the interface, and gathering feedback from potential users to better understand what works and what could be improved.

While I don't know exactly where Oddfellows will go in the future, I've enjoyed the process of developing something of my own and learning more about what it takes to build a product from the ground up.
